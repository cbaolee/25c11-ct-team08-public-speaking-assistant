A century later, when ENIAC, one of the first general-purpose electronic computers, was unveiled in 1946, the people who had programmed it were a team of six women. Grace Hopper developed one of the first compilers and helped shape COBOL, a language that still runs parts of the world's banking systems today. And Margaret Hamilton led the team that developed the onboard flight software for the Apollo missions, and helped popularize the term "software engineering."
