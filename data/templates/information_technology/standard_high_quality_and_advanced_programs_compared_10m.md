At many universities, these three programs lead to a degree in the same field, sometimes in the same faculty, with many of the same lecturers. But they differ in important ways: the curriculum, the language of instruction, class size, tuition fees, and the type of student they suit best. Let's look at each one, and then I'll give you some practical advice for choosing.

Before I begin, one quick note. Program names and structures differ between universities, and in recent years some universities have renamed or restructured these programs. So please treat what I describe today as the general picture, and always check the official information from the specific university you're interested in.

Let's start with the standard program.

The standard program is the main program of the faculty. It's usually the one with the largest number of students and the longest history.

Its biggest advantage is cost. Tuition fees are generally the lowest of the three. The curriculum provides a solid foundation in programming, mathematics, and core computer science, and most courses are taught in Vietnamese, which makes it easier to understand complex technical concepts, especially in the first years.

The trade-offs are usually larger classes, which means less individual attention, and less emphasis on English. Since English is extremely important in the IT industry, for documentation, tools, and international teams, students in the standard program often need to improve their English on their own initiative.

The standard program suits students who want a strong, affordable education, are comfortable learning independently, and are willing to build their English and practical skills outside the classroom.

Next, the high-quality program.

The high-quality program is usually based on the standard curriculum, but enhanced in several ways. Typically, that means smaller class sizes, better facilities, more courses or parts of courses taught in English, more practical projects, and more connections with companies through internships and guest lectures.

In return, tuition fees are significantly higher than the standard program.

The high-quality program suits students who want more support and a better learning environment, who want to improve their English during their studies, and whose families can afford the higher tuition.

And finally, the advanced program.

The advanced program, at universities that offer it, is often built on a curriculum adapted from a partner university abroad. Most or all courses are taught in English, and the program follows international standards in teaching methods and assessment. Some advanced programs also involve lecturers from partner universities and opportunities for exchange or further study abroad.

Its strengths are strong English skills, an international-style education, and good preparation for working in multinational companies or continuing to a master's degree overseas.

But it also has challenges. Tuition fees are usually the highest of the three. The workload can be demanding, especially in the first year, if your English isn't yet strong, because you'll be learning difficult technical subjects in a second language. And there may be a minimum English requirement for admission or for progressing into the main courses.

The advanced program suits students with good English, or a strong determination to reach that level quickly, who are aiming for an international career or study abroad.

So, let's compare the three side by side.

In terms of cost, the standard program is the most affordable, the high-quality program is in the middle, and the advanced program is usually the most expensive.

In terms of language, the standard program is mostly in Vietnamese, the high-quality program mixes Vietnamese and English, and the advanced program is mostly or fully in English.

In terms of class size and support, the standard program typically has larger classes, while the high-quality and advanced programs usually offer smaller classes and more individual attention.

And in terms of career outcomes, all three can lead to excellent jobs. The high-quality and advanced programs may offer more international exposure and stronger English, but they don't guarantee success, and a motivated standard-program student can reach the same level.

So, how should you choose? Five practical questions.

First, what can your family realistically afford, for all four years, not just the first? Never choose a program that will put serious financial pressure on your family. A great education is possible in every program.

Second, what's your current English level, and how much effort are you willing to put into it?

Third, what are your career goals? If you're aiming to work for international companies or study abroad, the advanced or high-quality programs may give you a head start. If you plan to work in a local company, or start your own business, the standard program can serve you perfectly well.

Fourth, how do you learn best? Do you need smaller classes and more guidance, or are you comfortable learning independently?

And fifth, what are the admission requirements? Scores and English requirements can differ significantly between programs.

If you visit the university before deciding, a few questions are worth asking directly. How many students are in a typical class in each program? Which courses are taught in English, and from which year? Is it possible to move from one program to another after the first year, and under what conditions? And what do recent graduates of each program actually do now? Current students are often the most honest source. Ask them what surprised them most in their first semester, and whether they'd choose the same program again.

One last point, and the most important one. The program you choose matters, but it matters less than what you do during your four years. Students who build real projects, take internships, join clubs and competitions, and keep improving their English often succeed regardless of which program they're in. The program gives you a starting environment. Your effort decides where you go from there.

Each program in a line: the standard program offers a solid, affordable foundation, mostly in Vietnamese. The high-quality program offers an enhanced environment with more English and support, at a higher cost. And the advanced program offers an international-style curriculum taught in English, at the highest cost and with the highest language demands. Choose based on your budget, your English, your goals, and how you learn, and always check the latest official information from the university.
