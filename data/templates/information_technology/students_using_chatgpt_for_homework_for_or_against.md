My name is {{ speaker_name }}, and today I'll argue a position that may sound like a compromise, but I believe is the only realistic one: students should be allowed to use ChatGPT and similar AI tools for homework, but only in ways that support learning, not replace it.

Start with why a total ban doesn't work.

First, it's practically impossible to enforce. These tools are free, available on every phone, and AI-detection tools are unreliable. They can miss AI-written text, and worse, they can wrongly accuse students who wrote their work themselves.

Second, students will need these skills. In many workplaces, AI tools are becoming everyday tools, like search engines or spreadsheets. Knowing how to use them well, and when not to trust them, is a skill schools should teach, not ignore.

And third, used well, AI can be an excellent tutor. A student who's stuck at midnight can ask for an explanation in simpler words, ask for an example, or get feedback on a draft. For students who can't afford private tutoring, that's a real benefit.

But I'm not arguing for unlimited use, and here's why.

The purpose of homework isn't to produce an answer. Teachers already know the answers. The purpose is to make your brain do the work, because that's how learning happens. If an AI writes your essay or solves your exercises, you get the grade, but you skip the learning. It's like sending a robot to the gym for you. The robot gets stronger, you don't.

There are also other risks. AI tools can be confidently wrong, inventing facts or sources that don't exist. And submitting AI-written work as your own is dishonest, unfair to classmates who did the work, and, at many schools, a serious academic violation.

So where's the line? I'd propose three simple rules.

Rule one: use AI to understand, not to produce. Asking "Can you explain this concept?" is fine. Asking "Write my assignment" is not.

Rule two: be transparent. If you used AI, say how, just as you'd cite any other source.

And rule three: you must be able to explain your work. If you can't explain an answer in your own words without the AI, it isn't really yours yet.

And teachers can help, by designing assignments that value the thinking process, such as drafts, reflections, oral explanations, and in-class work, and by clearly stating, for each assignment, what kind of AI use is allowed.

Some people will say: "But students will break the rules anyway, so why bother?" Some will, just as some students copied from friends long before AI existed. Rules don't stop everyone. But clear rules help the majority of students who want to do the right thing understand where the line is, and they give teachers a fair basis for decisions. That's far better than a ban everyone ignores, or no guidance at all.

So, to conclude. Banning AI is unrealistic and wastes its potential as a learning tool. Allowing it without limits destroys the purpose of homework. The answer is clear rules: AI as a tutor, not as a ghostwriter.

Because in the end, the question isn't whether ChatGPT can do your homework. It's whether you'll be able to do the work yourself when it really matters.
