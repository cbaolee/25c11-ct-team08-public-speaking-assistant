Congratulations. You've worked hard to be here, and you should be proud of that. And to the parents and families who may be listening, thank you for supporting these young people on their journey.

You're joining a field that shapes almost every part of modern life. The apps on your phone, the systems that run hospitals and banks, the tools that connect people across the world, all of them were built by people who, not so long ago, were sitting exactly where you are today.

{{ argument_faculty_introduction }}

Over the next few years, you'll learn programming, algorithms, data structures, databases, networks, and much more. Some of it will be exciting from the first day. Some of it will feel difficult, abstract, or even confusing. That's completely normal. Almost every experienced engineer you'll meet has struggled with a bug for hours, failed an exam at some point, or felt like everyone else understood something they didn't. What separates those who succeed isn't talent. It's persistence, curiosity, and the willingness to ask for help.

Three pieces of advice for your first year.

First, take the fundamentals seriously. Technologies change every few years, but the foundations, such as how to think logically, how to break a problem into parts, and how computers actually work, will serve you for your whole career. Courses that seem theoretical now are often the ones that matter most later.

Second, build things. Don't only study for exams. Write small programs for fun, solve problems that interest you, and join projects, clubs, and competitions. You learn programming by programming, and the mistakes you make on your own projects will teach you more than any lecture.

And third, don't do it alone. Make friends, form study groups, and help each other. Talk to your lecturers and teaching assistants. We're here to help you, and we're happy when you come to us with questions. {{ argument_support_resources }}

And one more thing, about artificial intelligence tools. Use them to help you understand, not to avoid understanding. If an AI writes your assignment, it learns nothing, and neither do you. Your goal here isn't to submit answers. It's to become someone who can solve problems.

The next few years will go by faster than you think. Make the most of them. Be curious, be kind to each other, and don't be afraid to fail along the way.
