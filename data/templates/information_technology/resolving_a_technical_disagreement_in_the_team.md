Something stands out, though. We actually agree on much more than it seems. We all want {{ shared_goal }}. Nobody disagrees on that. Where we differ is in how much weight we give to different things. One side is more focused on {{ option_a_priority }}, and the other is more focused on {{ option_b_priority }}. Both of those matter. The question is which one matters more for this particular situation, right now.

Rather than continuing to argue about which option is better in general, I'd like to suggest that we first agree on the criteria we're going to use to decide. Once we agree on the criteria, the decision usually becomes much easier, and much less personal.

A few candidate criteria, and please add or remove any. How well does each option meet our requirements for {{ key_requirement }}? How much time will it take to deliver, given our deadline of {{ deadline }}? How easy will it be to maintain over the next year or two? How well does it fit the skills our team already has? And how easy would it be to change our minds later if we turn out to be wrong?

That last one is important. Some decisions are like a one-way door: once you walk through, it's very hard to go back. Others are like a two-way door: if it doesn't work out, you can simply turn around. If this decision is a two-way door, we shouldn't spend another week debating it. We should pick one, try it, and adjust. If it's a one-way door, it's worth investing a bit more time to get it right.

Not because anyone's opinion is worth more, but because at some point, a good decision made on time is better than a perfect decision made too late.

Whatever we decide, and this part matters, once the decision is made, I'd like us to commit to it as a team, even those of us who argued for the other option. It's completely fine to disagree during the discussion. That's what makes our decisions better. But once we've decided, let's all put our full energy into making it work. Some teams call this "disagree and commit." And if the evidence later shows we made the wrong choice, nobody should say "I told you so." We'll just learn from it and adjust.

And let's write this decision down, including the options we considered, the criteria, and why we chose what we chose. Six months from now, when someone new joins the team and asks "why did we do it this way?", we'll have a clear answer.
