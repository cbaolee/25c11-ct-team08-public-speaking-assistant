So, who are we? {{ argument_company_overview }}

What makes this interesting for you as engineers is the kind of problems we work on. {{ argument_technical_challenges }}

Our main technologies are {{ argument_tech_stack }} But don't worry if you don't know all of them yet. We care much more about strong fundamentals and your ability to learn than about a perfect list of tools. A good developer can learn a new framework in a few weeks. Good problem-solving takes much longer to build.

Now, what it's like to work with us. {{ argument_culture_and_team_note }}

For students and fresh graduates, we currently have these opportunities. {{ argument_open_positions }}

And what new team members can expect. {{ argument_onboarding_and_growth }}

So, who are we looking for? It's less about grades and more about a few qualities. We look for people who have built something on their own or in a team, whether it's a class project, a personal app, or a hackathon entry, and who can explain it clearly. We look for curiosity: people who ask "why" and enjoy figuring things out. And we look for good teammates: people who communicate openly, accept feedback, and help others.

A quick tip that applies to any company here today, not just us. When you talk to a recruiter or engineer at a booth, don't just ask "Are you hiring?" Ask something specific, like "What does a typical week look like for a junior developer on your team?" or "What's the hardest technical problem your team solved recently?" You'll learn much more, and you'll be remembered.

If you're interested, here's what to do next. {{ argument_call_to_action }} And please come and talk to us at the booth. {{ argument_booth_engineers_note }} They're the best people to ask about what the daily work really looks like.

Good luck with your job search today. We hope to see some of you on our team soon!
