Hi everyone. I'm {{ speaker_name }}, and I'd like to convince you, in just a few minutes, that English might be the most valuable skill you can add to your IT toolkit, even more than the next programming language.

A simple fact: the world of technology runs in English.

Programming languages use English keywords: if, else, while, return. The official documentation for almost every major framework, library, and cloud service is written first, and sometimes only, in English. Error messages are in English. Most of the answers you'll find online when you're stuck are in English. And the newest information, release notes, research papers, and conference talks, usually appears in English long before it's translated, if it ever is.

So, a developer who reads English comfortably learns faster, solves problems faster, and stays up to date more easily. That's advantage number one: access to knowledge.

Advantage number two: opportunities. Many companies work with international clients or are part of global teams. Developers who can join meetings, write clear messages, and explain technical ideas in English are often the ones chosen for more interesting projects, promoted to lead roles, or offered positions abroad or remote jobs with international companies. {{ personal_example }}

And advantage number three: communication is part of engineering. Writing a clear bug report, a good pull request description, or technical documentation is a core professional skill. In global teams, all of that happens in English.

Now, the good news: you don't need perfect English. You need useful English. Four simple habits will build it.

First, read official documentation in English, even when a translation exists. Start slowly. It gets easier every week.

Second, write in English as part of your daily work: commit messages, code comments, and notes.

Third, listen to technical talks and podcasts in English, with subtitles at first.

And fourth, practice speaking: explain your project in English to a friend, record yourself, or join an English-speaking meetup. It will feel uncomfortable at first. That's normal.

Programming languages change every few years. English stays useful for your entire career. So start today. Read one page of documentation in English tonight.
