I want to talk to anyone here who's thinking, "I'd love to learn programming, but I don't have a degree, or money for courses, or a mentor." I was in that exact place. So here are five lessons that make the road shorter.

Lesson one: pick one path and stick with it. Almost every beginner jumps between languages, because every video says a different one is "the best." You learn a little of everything and can build nothing. Choose one goal, for example, building websites, and one language that fits it, and stay with it for at least a few months.

Lesson two: build things, don't just watch things. Tutorials feel productive, but you can watch a hundred of them and still freeze in front of an empty screen. Some people call this "tutorial hell." The way out is to build your own small projects, even ugly ones. A to-do list, a personal website, a tool that solves a small problem in your own life. You learn the most when you're stuck and have to figure it out.

Lesson three: learn the fundamentals, even if nobody forces you to. Without a university, it's easy to skip things like data structures, algorithms, or how the internet works. But those are exactly what interviewers ask about, and what make you a stronger developer. There are excellent free resources online for all of them.

Lesson four: don't learn alone. Join online communities, local meetups, and open-source projects. Ask questions, and later, answer other people's questions. Teaching someone else is one of the best ways to learn.

And lesson five: be consistent, not intense. One hour every day beats ten hours once a week. My turning point was {{ turning_point }}

And one more thing: when you start looking for a job, you'll need to prove your skills without a diploma. So put your best projects on GitHub, write a short, clear description for each one, and be ready to explain every line of code you wrote. Your portfolio is your diploma.

Being self-taught isn't the easy path. There's nobody to tell you what to do next, and some days you'll feel completely lost. But it's absolutely possible. Many developers working today taught themselves.

Start today. Pick one small project, and write your first line of code tonight.
