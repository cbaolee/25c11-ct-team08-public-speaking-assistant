First, the underlying torque-balance principle behind why staggered oars twist a narrow boat.

In a standard alternating rig—where port and starboard oars alternate one-by-one from bow to stern—the forward-most oar sits closer to the bow tip than the opposing oar on the opposite side, giving it a longer lever arm about the boat's center of mass. When all eight athletes pull simultaneously, that slight lever-arm asymmetry creates a rhythmic yawing torque that pushes the bow slightly off line, forcing the coxswain to apply drag-inducing rudder corrections. By placing two consecutive rowers on the same side in the middle of the shell—known as a tandem or bucket rig—engineers equalize the sum of lever-arm moments between port and starboard.
