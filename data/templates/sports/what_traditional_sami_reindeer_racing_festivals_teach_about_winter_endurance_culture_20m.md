Even appreciative visitors fall prey to five common misconceptions about Sámi reindeer racing festivals.

Misstep one: assuming the racer rides on the reindeer's back like a horse jockey. Reindeer spines are not built to carry adult riders; in Sámi racing, the athlete wears skis—or sits in a traditional wooden pulka sled—and is pulled smoothly behind the reindeer via a breast harness.

Misstep two: thinking reindeer are slow, plodding winter animals. Over sprint distances of two hundred to one thousand meters, trained racing reindeer regularly exceed fifty kilometers per hour—comparable to thoroughbred horses on snow.

Misstep three: believing racers steer with two reins and a metal bit in the animal's mouth. Sámi reindeer racing uses a soft halter and a single leather rein, relying on gentle balance cues and trust rather than forceful mechanical control.

Misstep four: assuming reindeer racing festivals are staged solely for winter tourists. While visitors are warmly welcomed, the festivals remain vital annual community reunions, trading markets, and sporting championships for Sámi families across four countries.

Misstep five: thinking anyone can enter a championship heat without prior herding connection. Traditional championship circuits preserve deep cultural ties to reindeer-herding communities, ensuring every competing animal receives year-round knowledgeable pastoral care.
