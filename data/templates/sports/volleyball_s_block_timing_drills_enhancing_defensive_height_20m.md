Drill 1: The Static Timing Cue

The purpose of this drill is to create a baseline sense of when to jump relative to the hitter’s arm swing. We’ll use a stationary hitter and a fixed block line. The hitter will stand in a ready position, and the blocker will stand just behind the block line, holding a marker in each hand. The hitter will then perform a set, and the blocker will attempt to jump so that the marker aligns with the ball at the moment of contact.

Execution  
1. The hitter takes a normal approach, lifts the ball, and sets it to a spot in the middle of the net.  
2. The blocker watches the hitter’s arm swing and jumps when the hitter’s hand passes the set point.  
3. After each set, the blocker lands, and the coach provides immediate feedback on whether the marker was too high, too low, or just right.  

Key Points  
Focus on the moment the hitter’s hand passes the set point; this is the cue for the blocker.  
Keep the arms relaxed until the last possible moment.  
The blocker should aim to have the marker just above the ball when the hitter’s arm is fully extended.  

This drill is simple, but it establishes a mental model of the timing relationship between the hitter and the blocker.
Drill 2: The Moving Hitter Drill

Once the blocker has a baseline timing cue, we add movement to the hitter. The hitter now runs a short approach, sets, and attacks. The blocker must adjust for the hitter’s speed and the resulting change in timing.

Execution  
1. The hitter runs a normal approach and sets the ball.  
2. The blocker follows the same cue as in Drill 1 but must account for the hitter’s speed.  
3. After each hit, the blocker lands and the coach provides feedback.  

Key Points  
The blocker should adjust the takeoff point slightly earlier if the hitter’s approach is faster.  
The blocker should maintain the same arm swing cadence as in Drill 1, but the timing relative to the hitter’s approach changes.  
Communication with the other blocker is critical; they must agree on who will take the hit if both are in range.  

This drill introduces the concept that timing is relative not only to the hitter’s arm swing but also to the hitter’s approach speed.
Drill 3: The Full‑Team Blocking Drill

The final drill integrates the entire blocking unit. Two blockers stand on either side of the net, and a hitter approaches from the left side. The blockers must decide who takes the hit, communicate, and execute the block with perfect timing.

Execution  
1. The hitter runs a standard approach and attacks.  
2. The blockers watch the hitter’s arm swing and the approach.  
3. The blockers call out “left” or “right” and time their jumps accordingly.  
4. After each hit, the blockers land and reset.  

Key Points  
The blockers should maintain a tight stance to minimize the time between takeoff and arm swing.  
The communication call must be clear and consistent.  
The blockers must adjust their timing based on the hitter’s speed and the ball’s trajectory.  

This drill simulates a real match scenario where the blockers must work together to adjust timing on the fly.
