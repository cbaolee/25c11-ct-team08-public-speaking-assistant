Even curious aquatic sports fans fall into five common misconceptions when encountering underwater hockey for the first time.

Misstep one: assuming players wear heavy scuba tanks on their backs during matches. Underwater hockey is played strictly on breath-hold using only mask, snorkel, and fins—only the underwater referees wear scuba gear so they can hover continuously near the bottom.

Misstep two: thinking the puck floats through mid-water like a soccer ball. A regulation underwater hockey puck weighs roughly one point three to one point five kilograms—three pounds—with a dense lead core encased in protective plastic so it hugs the tiled pool floor reliably.

Misstep three: believing players are allowed to bat or push the puck with their free hand. Touching or shielding the puck with the non-stick hand is an immediate foul; players wear a thick silicone-padded glove on their playing hand solely to protect knuckles from scraping against pool tiles.

Misstep four: assuming underwater hockey is a violent contact sport hidden below water. Strict non-contact rules prohibit holding, pushing, or swimming over opponents—making agility, fin propulsion, and stick-handling finesse the decisive skills.

Misstep five: thinking matches can only be watched properly by wearing a mask and floating face-down in the pool yourself. Modern multi-camera underwater broadcasts and high-clarity championship pools allow fans in dry seats or living rooms to follow every stick-flick in crystal-clear high definition.
