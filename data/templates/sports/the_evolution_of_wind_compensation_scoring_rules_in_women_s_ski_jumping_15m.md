Finally, three questions that come up very often when audiences explore ski jumping wind-compensation scoring.

"Why does a ski jumper want a headwind instead of a tailwind when jumping down a mountain?" Because a ski jumper glides forward like an airplane wing, wind blowing uphill into their face increases relative airspeed over the skis and generates upward aerodynamic lift—carrying them farther down the landing slope.

"What is a telemark landing, and why do style judges deduct points if a jumper lands with both feet flat side-by-side?" A telemark landing—one foot stepped smoothly ahead of the other with knees flexed like a lunge—absorbs steep landing impact gracefully and demonstrates complete mid-air control, whereas a flat two-foot landing incurs automatic style deductions.
