Stage two: delaying laminar-to-turbulent transition along the forward hull.

Near the pointed bow of a racing shell, water flows smoothly in orderly, parallel sheets—laminar flow—generating remarkably low friction drag. Mirror-smooth, blemish-free hull finishes extend the low-drag laminar flow zone farther aft along the bow sections, saving measurable watts across five hundred strokes.

Traditional boat waxes and untreated clear-coats have moderate surface energy that allows water droplets to spread and cling tightly to the hull.
