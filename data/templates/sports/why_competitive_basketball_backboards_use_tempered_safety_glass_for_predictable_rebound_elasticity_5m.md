First, the underlying material-physics principle behind tempered glass rigidity and uniform restitution.

Unlike clear polycarbonate or acrylic plastic boards—which flex noticeably upon ball impact, absorb kinetic energy, and scratch cloudy over time—thermally tempered glass is heated to over six hundred degrees Celsius and rapidly air-quenched, locking the outer surfaces into high compression against an inner tension core. That high surface modulus gives tempered glass an exceptionally crisp, uniform coefficient of restitution across every square centimeter of the face.
