Part two: horizontal hip abduction and external rotation clearing the top bar.

Because an elite hurdler's hips pass only inches higher than the top edge of the barrier, pulling the back knee straight forward underneath the body would slam the shin directly into the crossbar. Even passionate track-and-field fans fall into five common blind spots when watching sprint hurdle trail-leg mechanics.

Misstep one: assuming the trail leg simply drags passively behind the runner while the front leg does all the work. In reality, the trail leg travels at peak angular velocity around the side of the barrier and powers the immediate first sprint stride upon landing.

Misstep two: thinking trailing the back foot around the outside edge of the hurdle bar is a legal shortcut to avoid lifting the leg high. Most trail-leg clips occur because the athlete lifted their trail leg too early at takeoff or let their chest pop upright prematurely—dropping the back hip downward over the bar.
