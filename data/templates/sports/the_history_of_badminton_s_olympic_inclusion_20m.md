The sport of badminton has a long, winding lineage that dates back centuries. While it is often associated with the modern Olympic Games, its roots can be traced to ancient games played in India, China, and Persia. These early versions were played with a shuttlecock, or "bird," and a racket, but they were very different from what we recognize today. It wasn’t until the 19th century, when British colonial officers stationed in India brought their version of the game back to England, that badminton began to take on a more standardized form. In England, the sport quickly gained popularity among the upper classes and soon spread to schools and clubs across the country. By the early 20th century, the Badminton Association of England had been formed, and the sport was codified with official rules and a governing body.

Despite its growing popularity, badminton’s journey to Olympic inclusion was far from straightforward. In the early 20th century, the International Olympic Committee (IOC) was largely focused on sports that had widespread, organized participation worldwide. Badminton, although popular in certain regions, did not yet have the global reach or infrastructure that the IOC required. Additionally, the sport faced competition from other racket sports such as tennis and squash, which had already secured Olympic spots. These factors contributed to badminton’s delayed recognition as an Olympic sport.

It wasn’t until the 1970s that badminton’s prospects began to change. During this period, the sport experienced a surge in international participation, with more countries forming national federations and participating in world championships. The International Badminton Federation (IBF), now known as the Badminton World Federation (BWF), played a pivotal role in this development. The IBF worked tirelessly to promote the sport, organize world championships, and build a case for its Olympic inclusion. By the early 1980s, the sport had gained enough traction to be considered a serious contender for the Olympic program.

The turning point came in 1981, when the IOC announced that badminton would be added as a demonstration sport at the 1988 Seoul Olympics. This decision was the culmination of years of lobbying and advocacy by badminton’s governing bodies, as well as the growing interest in the sport among Olympic audiences. The demonstration event at Seoul was a success, showcasing the speed, agility, and skill required to play badminton at the highest level. This success paved the way for the sport’s official inclusion in the 1992 Barcelona Olympics, marking a milestone that many fans and players had dreamed of for decades.
