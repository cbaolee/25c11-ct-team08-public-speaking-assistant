First, the underlying composite-engineering principle behind pairing stiff carbon with damping arylate.

Pure carbon fiber is extraordinarily stiff and lightweight—expanding the effective sweet spot across the entire blade face so off-center hits rebound with the exact same speed as dead-center hits, yet pure carbon also transmits sharp, harsh vibration into the fingers and launches balls too quickly for delicate short-game control. Arylate—a tough aromatic polyamide fiber similar to ballistic Kevlar—possesses high internal vibration-damping capacity and soft elastic flex. Second, how carbon-arylate blade architecture plays out in real tournament competition.

Modern championship players choose deliberately between outer-carbon constructions—where the composite layer sits directly beneath a thin outer wood veneer for immediate, explosive rebound—and inner-carbon constructions, where the composite layer sits deeper next to the central core wood so soft touch shots behave like an all-wood blade until a heavy swing engages the inner carbon catapult.
