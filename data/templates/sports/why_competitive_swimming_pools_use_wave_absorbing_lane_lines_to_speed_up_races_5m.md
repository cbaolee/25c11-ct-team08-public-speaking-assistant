First, the underlying fluid-mechanics principle behind breaking up surface wave energy.

Surface waves carry kinetic energy along the air-water boundary, and when an unbroken wave crest strikes a swimmer's shoulders or head, it spikes hydrodynamic form drag and disrupts breathing rhythm. Modern anti-wave lane lines consist of hundreds of independently spinning, finned polyethylene discs strung along a tensioned stainless-steel cable. When a lateral bow wave hits those slotted, rotating turbine discs, the angled fins split the wave crest into tiny counter-rotating micro-eddies, converting coherent wave momentum into harmless localized swirl.
