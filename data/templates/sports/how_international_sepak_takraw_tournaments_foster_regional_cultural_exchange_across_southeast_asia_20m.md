Even enthusiastic new viewers fall prey to five common misconceptions about sepak takraw.

Misstep one: assuming players use wooden or bamboo racquets to strike the ball. Sepak takraw is played strictly with feet, knees, chest, and head—never with hands, arms, or racquets of any kind.

Misstep two: thinking the ball is a hard, solid wooden sphere. A regulation sepak takraw ball is a lightweight, hollow woven sphere—traditionally crafted from interlaced rattan strips and today made from woven synthetic polymer—weighing roughly one hundred seventy to one hundred eighty grams so players can strike it bare-footed or in thin court shoes without injury.

Misstep three: believing every player on a three-person regu performs the exact same role. Positions are highly specialized: the tekong anchors the back circle and delivers high-speed serves, the feeder controls first-touch passes and precision sets, and the killer executes aerial spikes and net blocks.

Misstep four: assuming the sport originated in only one single Southeast Asian country. Historical records show woven-ball foot games flourishing across Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Myanmar, the Philippines, and neighboring kingdoms for centuries—making sepak takraw a genuinely shared regional heritage.

Misstep five: thinking aerial bicycle spikes are purely acrobatic showboating. Inverting upside down mid-air elevates the striking foot well above the five-foot net tape, creating steep downward spike angles that would be geometrically impossible from an upright kick.
