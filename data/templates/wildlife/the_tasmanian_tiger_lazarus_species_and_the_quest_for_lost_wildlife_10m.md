# How a Lie Killed an Apex Predator

How did the top marsupial predator of an entire island go from thousands of healthy animals in the wild to zero in barely a century?

Not by accident. **We paid people to wipe them out.**

When European sheep farmers settled in {{ island_name }} in the 1800s, sheep started disappearing or dying from stray dog attacks, bad weather, and theft. Looking for an easy scapegoat, wool corporations and politicians blamed the shy, nocturnal {{ target_extinct_marsupial }} -- painting it in newspapers as a bloodthirsty sheep-killer and offering a **government bounty of {{ bounty_amount_statistic }}** for every dead adult scalp brought to the courthouse.

Between {{ bounty_start_year }} and {{ bounty_end_year }}, hunters turned in **over {{ official_bounties_paid_count }} {{ target_extinct_marsupial }} bodies** for cash, while thousands more were snared and poisoned.

Years later -- long after the last {{ target_extinct_marsupial }} was dead in a museum drawer -- biomechanical engineers CT-scanned their skulls and tested the strength of their jaws.

> The result was heartbreaking: the {{ target_extinct_marsupial }}'s jaws were actually **too weak and narrow to bring down an adult sheep**; it had evolved to hunt small wallabies, possums, and birds! We exterminated an entire evolutionary family over a crime it wasn't even built to commit.

# The Danger of Crying "Extinct" Too Soon -- or Too Late

Why does the line between "Critically Endangered" and "Extinct" on the Red List matter so much?

Because of what biologists call the **Romeo Error**.

Just like Romeo in Shakespeare's play thought Juliet was dead and gave up hope five minutes before she woke up, if conservationists declare a rare animal like the **{{ critically_endangered_missing_species }}** "Extinct" too early, governments immediately **cancel protection for its forest, pull out the rangers, and let loggers bulldoze its last hiding place** -- actually *causing* the extinction of the few survivors that were still hiding inside!
