# Masters of Water and Heat

How does a warm-blooded mammal or a delicate reptile survive where there is no standing water to drink for {{ drought_duration_statistic }}? Desert specialists -- what biologists call **xerocoles** -- have evolved some of the most ingenious adaptations on the planet.

Instead, it manufactures its own water internally through the metabolic oxidation of dry seeds from {{ desert_plant_species }}, while its specialized nasal passages cool the air it exhales to recapture {{ moisture_recapture_percentage }} of its breath's moisture.

Or consider the {{ large_desert_ungulate_species }} roaming the gravel plains of {{ desert_subregion_name }}. For most mammals, a core body temperature above {{ normal_mammal_fever_limit }} causes fatal brain damage. Yet the {{ large_desert_ungulate_species }} can allow its body temperature to rise to {{ hyperthermia_tolerance_statistic }} during the heat of the day so it doesn't lose precious water by sweating. How does its brain survive? Through a miraculous web of blood vessels in its head called the **carotid rete**, which acts as a biological radiator to keep blood flowing to the brain {{ brain_cooling_differential }} cooler than the rest of its body.

Even the tiny {{ fog_basking_invertebrate_or_reptile }} has mastered atmospheric engineering, climbing to the crests of dunes at dawn and tilting its body into the coastal breeze so microscopic grooves on its skin condense fog droplets and channel them directly into its mouth.

# Living on the Razor's Edge

Because desert wildlife is so impressive at surviving extreme heat, people often assume that deserts will be immune to global climate change. In reality, our physiological field data shows the exact opposite: desert ecosystems are operating closer to their **absolute thermal ceiling** than almost any other biome on Earth.

In the tropical rainforest, an animal might have a safety margin of several degrees before heat stress becomes lethal. In {{ desert_region_name }}, species like the {{ desert_bird_or_lizard_species }} are already living right on the razor's edge.
