# Introduction: The Tree That Drinks Saltwater

Try taking a bucket of salty ocean water and pouring it over the roses or the lawn in your front yard. Within three days, every single plant will shrivel up and turn brown. For {{ freshwater_plant_percentage }} of all land plants on Earth, salt is pure poison.

> Local fishermen call them **"walking trees"** because their arching prop roots look like hundreds of legs striding straight out into the sea -- building new land where yesterday there was only open water.

# Snorkels, Filters, and Flying Torpedoes

How on earth does a {{ target_mangrove_species }} thrive in a place that would kill any oak or pine tree in a week? Through three brilliant pieces of natural engineering.

First, how do its roots breathe when they are buried deep in stinky, oxygen-free mud? The tree grows thousands of little vertical **root snorkels** -- called pneumatophores -- that poke straight up out of the mud like a bed of pencils to gulp fresh air whenever the tide goes out.

Second, how does it drink seawater without dying of thirst? The membranes in the roots of a {{ target_mangrove_species }} act like an ultra-fine **desalination plant**, blocking {{ salt_filtration_percentage }} of the salt right at the door so fresh water flows up into the trunk. Other species sweat the extra salt out through special glands on their leaves so you can literally lick pure salt crystals off the foliage.

Third, how do you drop a seed into swirling tidal water without it drowning or washing out to sea? You don't drop a seed at all! The {{ target_mangrove_species }} practices **live birth**: the seed germinates while still hanging on the mother branch, growing into a long, green, spear-shaped baby tree called a **propagule**. When it finally drops, it can float horizontally for {{ floating_survival_months }} until it hits shallow mud, flips vertically to point its root end down, and plants itself in hours.

Today, everyone wants to plant mangroves for carbon credits. Yet when we audited mass tree-planting projects across {{ audit_region_name }}, we found a shocking secret: **up to {{ failed_planting_percentage }} of planted mangrove seedlings die within two years**.

Why? Because well-meaning groups go out at low tide and stick thousands of {{ target_mangrove_species }} spears into open, wave-battered mudflats where mangroves never naturally grew in the first place, instead of fixing the blocked tidal creeks where mangroves *want* to grow.

In {{ successful_restoration_site }}, working with women's shellfish collectives led by {{ community_leader_name }}, we flipped the script to **Ecological Mangrove Restoration**. Instead of planting monoculture rows on bare beaches, we took shovels and opened up the dikes of abandoned {{ aquaculture_commodity }} ponds so the natural daily tide could flow back in and out.

When we work *with* the tide instead of against it, the walking trees march right back in -- guarding our coasts, feeding our fishers, and cooling our globe. Thank you.
