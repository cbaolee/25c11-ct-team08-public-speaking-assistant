# Introduction: The Biggest Con Artists in the Woods

We usually think of flowers as the most honest, generous traders in nature. The deal is simple: a flower puts on a bright colorful sign, smells nice, and offers a sweet sip of sugary **nectar**. A bee or a butterfly stops by for lunch, gets dusted with pollen, and carries it to the next flower. Everybody wins.

Then you meet the **wild orchids**.

With over {{ orchid_species_count_statistic }} wild species around the globe, orchids are the biggest family of flowering plants on Earth. And fully **one-third of all wild orchids are total con artists**. They don't make a single drop of nectar. Instead, they trick insects into doing their work for free through sheer **mastery of disguise**.

> If Hollywood ever handed out an Oscar for special effects and method acting, a wild orchid in {{ study_meadow_or_forest }} would win every single year.
