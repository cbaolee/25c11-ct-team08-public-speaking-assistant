# A Horn Made of Hair and Fingernails

Unlike an elephant's tusk or a deer's antler, a rhino's horn isn't made of bone or ivory, and it isn't attached to its skull.

It is made of **compressed keratin fibers** -- the exact same protein as your hair and fingernails -- glued together by calcium and melanin. In fact, if you saw a rhino's horn off a few centimeters above the skin while the animal is safely asleep under anesthesia, it doesn't hurt the rhino any more than a haircut hurts you, and the horn **grows right back** at about {{ horn_regrowth_cm_per_year }} centimeters a year!

Yet on the international black market in {{ trafficking_destination_regions }}, a single kilogram of ground-up rhino horn sells for **up to {{ black_market_price_per_kg }}** -- more per gram than gold, platinum, or cocaine.

Why are people paying more than the price of gold for ground-up fingernails?

For two completely different, equally tragic reasons. First, a persistent myth that powdered rhino horn can cure everything from a hangover to terminal cancer -- even though clinical pharmacology labs tested it decades ago and proved that taking rhino horn is medically identical to **biting your own fingernails**.

Second, and even bigger today, whole carved rhino horns and bracelets have become **illegal luxury status symbols** among corrupt elites who display them like stolen art to show off their wealth.

Because of that manufactured greed, between {{ poaching_surge_start_year }} and {{ poaching_surge_end_year }}, heavily armed poaching syndicates using night-vision scopes, silencers, and helicopters slaughtered **over {{ total_rhinos_poached_decade }} rhinos** across {{ african_range_states }} -- killing one rhino every {{ hours_between_poaching_deaths }} hours at the peak of the crisis.

# Moving Giants Upside Down by Helicopter

Third, to rescue rhinos from high-risk border zones and start brand-new breeding populations in safe sanctuaries across {{ safe_recipient_countries }}, wildlife vets pioneered **aerial translocation**.

It looks wild the first time you see it in the air: after gently darting a {{ rhino_weight_tons }}-ton **{{ black_or_white_rhino_species }}**, vets blindfold it, put giant earmuffs over its ears, and lift it **upside down by its ankles** beneath a helicopter for a ten-minute flight to a waiting transport truck!

Wait -- why hang a rhino upside down by its feet instead of putting it on a stretcher on its side?

Our veterinary pulmonary research proved that when a heavy rhino lies on its side in a sling, its massive guts press against its lungs and restrict oxygen -- whereas hanging upside down by its feet for ten minutes actually stretches its spine and lets its lungs **breathe better** than lying down, winning our veterinary team an Ig Nobel Prize for science that looks funny and saves lives!
