# Introduction: The Smallest Whale With the Biggest Smile

Picture a tiny, shy porpoise no longer than a ten-year-old child, swimming in the warm, shallow waters at the very top of {{ gulf_location_name }}.

Around its eyes are dark black rings like a panda's, and around its lips is a curved black line that makes it look like it is **smiling** at you.

Local fishermen call her the **{{ target_porpoise_species }}** -- Spanish for *"the little cow."* She lives in only one tiny patch of water on planet Earth, smaller than {{ range_size_comparison_city }}.

And as I stand on this stage tonight, there are **around {{ surviving_individuals_count }} of them left alive in the entire world**. Not {{ surviving_individuals_count }} thousand. Just **{{ surviving_individuals_count }}**.

> The {{ target_porpoise_species }} is the most endangered marine mammal on Earth -- sitting on the absolute knife-edge of the Red List.

# Caught in a War Aimed at Someone Else

Here is the heartbreaking twist: **nobody has ever hunted a {{ target_porpoise_species }} on purpose**. Nobody eats their meat, and nobody buys their fins.

Every single {{ target_porpoise_species }} that dies is an accidental victim of a black-market crime aimed at a completely different animal: a giant endangered fish living in the exact same water called the **{{ target_fish_species }}**.

Why are criminal cartels setting miles of illegal **gillnets** at night to catch the {{ target_fish_species }}? Not even for the fish itself, but solely for its **swim bladder** -- the gas bag inside the fish -- which smugglers dry and sell on the black market in {{ destination_market_region }} for up to **{{ swim_bladder_price_per_kilo }} a kilogram**, earning it the nickname *"aquatic cocaine."*

Because a {{ target_porpoise_species }} is almost the exact same size as a {{ target_fish_species }}, when it swims into one of those invisible nylon walls in the dark, it gets tangled, cannot swim up to the surface to breathe air, and **drowns in minutes**. Between {{ decline_start_year }} and {{ decline_end_year }}, illegal nets wiped out **{{ population_loss_percentage }} of the entire species**.

# Why ten Is Not Zero

When our acoustic and visual survey team went out into the **Zero Tolerance Area** of {{ gulf_location_name }} in {{ recent_survey_year }}, critics told us to give up. They said, *"With only {{ surviving_individuals_count }} animals left, aren't they doomed by inbreeding anyway?"*

Then two things happened that proved the critics wrong.

First, genomic scientists sequenced the DNA of the {{ target_porpoise_species }} and discovered that because this species has always lived in a tiny, naturally small population for **{{ evolutionary_history_years }} years**, it purged its harmful genetic mutations thousands of years ago!

Second, when our observers looked through high-powered binoculars from our research ship, the {{ surviving_individuals_count }} {{ target_porpoise_species }} we spotted were plump, healthy, actively feeding -- and swimming right beside a mother was **a brand-new, healthy baby calf**!

# Concrete Blocks and Net-Free Livelihoods

The {{ target_porpoise_species }} hasn't given up on living; she just needs us to get the nets out of her home.

In {{ enforcement_year }}, working alongside the Navy and conservation ships in {{ gulf_location_name }}, authorities dropped **{{ concrete_blocks_count }} massive concrete blocks fitted with steel hooks** onto the seafloor of the Zero Tolerance Area to snag and rip apart any illegal gillnet dragged into the sanctuary, **cutting illegal netting inside the core zone by {{ net_reduction_percentage }}**.

Today, by supporting local fishing families in {{ local_fishing_towns }} to transition to **vaquita-safe handline gear** and sustainable aquaculture, we are fighting for every single breath of those last {{ surviving_individuals_count }} survivors.

> Ten is a terrifyingly small number -- but **ten is not zero**. As long as mothers are still having calves in {{ gulf_location_name }}, the fight to keep the little smiling porpoise off the extinct list is still on. Thank you.
