# Example Two: Ocean Fish as Deep-Sea Carbon Elevators

Now jump from the dry savanna into the **open ocean** -- which has absorbed **{{ ocean_heat_absorption_percentage }} of all the excess heat** and nearly a third of the carbon dioxide humanity has ever produced.

We already know how great whales fertilize surface plankton with their iron-rich plumes. Yet what about the **billions of ordinary bony fish** -- **{{ mesopelagic_and_coastal_fish_examples }}** -- swimming in the ocean?

Here is a cool biological secret about marine fish: to keep from dehydrating in salty seawater, a marine fish has to drink seawater all day long. Inside its gut, the fish combines the calcium and magnesium in that seawater with the carbon dioxide produced by its own body to manufacture little white chalky pellets of **calcium carbonate**, which it poops out continuously!

Those chalky pellets -- along with fast-sinking fecal pellets from deep-sea **lanternfish** that swim up to the surface to eat every night and swim **{{ vertical_migration_depth_meters }} meters back down into the dark** before sunrise -- sink to the deep ocean floor like stones, locking away **over {{ fish_carbon_billion_tons }} billion tons of carbon every year**!

When industrial trawlers overfish the ocean and drag heavy metal nets across the continental shelf, they don't just kill the living carbon elevators; **bottom trawling stirs up {{ trawling_carbon_emissions_gigatons }} gigaton of ancient carbon buried in seafloor mud every year** -- emitting as much carbon dioxide as **the entire global aviation industry**!

# Example Three: Natural Armor Against Floods, Hurricanes, and Megafires

Protecting wild ecosystems and their animals doesn't just stop global warming from getting worse (**mitigation**); it is also our single best **climate armor** to protect human cities and farms from the extreme weather that is *already* here (**adaptation**)!

Think about what climate change feels like to a human family: it feels like **floods, storm surges, droughts, and heatwaves**.

Now compare how a degraded landscape versus a wild, animal-rich landscape handles a climate disaster:

* **Along our coasts:** During Hurricane {{ hurricane_example_name }}, coastal counties protected by intact **marshes, oyster reefs, and mangrove forests** suffered **{{ storm_damage_savings_billions }} billion dollars less property damage** than counties that had replaced their wetlands with concrete seawalls. And unlike a concrete wall that starts cracking the day you pour it and costs millions to raise when sea levels rise, a living oyster reef or mangrove forest **repairs itself for free after a storm and grows upward automatically** to match rising seas!
* **In our mountain valleys:** When a once-in-a-century rainstorm hits a steep watershed that has been clear-cut and stripped of its **{{ wetland_engineer_animal }}** and old-growth trees, the water races down the bare slopes like a concrete chute and drowns the town below in hours. In the valley next door where {{ wetland_engineer_animal }} dams, deep forest soils, and root networks are intact, the landscape soaks up the deluge like a giant sponge and releases it gently over weeks!
* **Inside our cities:** Neighborhoods with vibrant urban forests and green wildlife corridors stay **up to {{ urban_cooling_degrees_celsius }} degrees Celsius cooler** during lethal summer heatwaves than treeless concrete neighborhoods just ten blocks away -- saving thousands of elderly lives without burning a single kilowatt of air-conditioning!
