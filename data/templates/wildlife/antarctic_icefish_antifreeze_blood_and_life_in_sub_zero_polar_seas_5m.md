# Introduction: Swimming Inside a Slushie

What happens if you put a normal goldfish or a tropical tuna into a tank of seawater cooled down to **minus {{ subzero_water_temp_celsius }} degrees Celsius** -- the normal year-round temperature of the Southern Ocean around Antarctica?

Within sixty seconds, the fish freezes solid and dies. Because seawater is salty, the ocean around Antarctica stays liquid down to **minus 1.9 degrees Celsius** -- while the fresh water inside a normal fish's blood freezes at **minus 0.8 degrees**.

Yet when we drop an underwater camera through a hole in the Antarctic ice shelf at {{ antarctic_research_station }}, the freezing water underneath is packed with fish swimming happily through clouds of ice crystals!

And the weirdest of them all is the **{{ target_icefish_species }}** -- the only animal with a backbone on planet Earth that has **completely clear, white blood**.

> Cut your finger, and your blood is bright red. Look inside a {{ target_icefish_species }}, and its blood looks like **water**, its gills are creamy white, and its heart is the size of a golf ball!

# How to Survive With Zero Red Blood Cells

Wait a second -- every high school biology book says no vertebrate can live without **hemoglobin**, the red iron protein in our blood that carries oxygen from our lungs to our muscles. If a human lost their hemoglobin, they would pass out in seconds.

How did the {{ target_icefish_species }} throw away its red blood cells {{ evolutionary_loss_million_years }} million years ago and survive?

First, freezing cold water holds **way more dissolved oxygen** than warm tropical water -- and in sub-zero cold, red blood cells actually make blood thick and syrupy like cold motor oil, which is exhausting to pump.

By ditching red blood cells altogether, the {{ target_icefish_species }} turned its blood into a thin, easy-flowing liquid that absorbs oxygen straight through its scaleless skin and giant gills, pushed through extra-wide blood vessels by a **giant slow-beating heart** four times bigger than a normal fish's!

# Natural Antifreeze in Their Veins

And why doesn't that watery blood turn into an ice cube at minus {{ subzero_water_temp_celsius }} degrees?

Because Antarctic fish brew their own **biological antifreeze proteins**! Whenever a microscopic ice crystal tries to form inside their body, these special proteins wrap around the crystal like a Velcro jacket and stop it from growing any bigger.

# A Cold Specialist in a Warming Ocean

Today, doctors study the {{ target_icefish_species }} to learn how to treat human **osteoporosis** and keep donor organs alive on ice for transplants.

Yet the very adaptations that made the {{ target_icefish_species }} the king of the freezing sea now put it in danger. Because it has no red blood cells to carry extra oxygen when the water warms up, a temperature rise of just **{{ lethal_warming_degrees }} degrees Celsius** leaves it gasping for breath.

In {{ breeding_discovery_year }}, our deep-sea cameras in the {{ antarctic_sea_name }} discovered the largest fish nursery on Earth: **{{ nest_count_statistic }} {{ target_icefish_species }} nests** guarded by devoted fathers on the seafloor. By turning that nursery into an international **Marine Protected Area** off-limits to commercial trawlers, we can keep the white-blooded wonders of Antarctica thriving under the ice. Thank you.
