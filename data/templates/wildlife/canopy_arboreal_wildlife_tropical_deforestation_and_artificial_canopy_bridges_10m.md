# AScar Across the Sky

Because {{ target_arboreal_species }} evolved to move exclusively through interlocking tree branches, a gap of just {{ critical_gap_width_statistic }} in the canopy might as well be a canyon miles wide.

When energy companies and highway builders cut a corridor through {{ study_region_name }} in {{ infrastructure_construction_year }} to install {{ linear_infrastructure_type }}, they cleared a strip only {{ clearing_width_statistic }} wide. Those desperate enough to climb down and crawl across the exposed ground were killed by {{ ground_hazards_list }}.

Within {{ isolation_timeframe }}, the small sub-populations trapped in forest fragments began suffering from inbreeding and parasite buildup.

# Reconnecting the Treetops

When our team approached {{ infrastructure_company_name }} with this data, we proposed two solutions. For future projects, we advocated for **canopy-level planning** -- routing pipelines and roads around tall emergent trees so natural branch bridges remain intact across the right-of-way.

And where the canopy had already been severed across {{ severed_corridor_length }}, we worked with local arborists from {{ local_community_name }} to design and rig {{ bridge_count_statistic }} **artificial canopy bridges** using UV-resistant marine rope, bamboo rungs, and guided liana vines at a height of {{ bridge_height_statistic }}.

> Each bridge cost less than {{ single_bridge_cost_statistic }} -- a fraction of a percent of the highway budget -- yet it restored a vital lifeline in the sky.

# Watching the Forest Cross Over

At first, the animals were cautious. For {{ habituation_period_timeframe }}, our solar-powered motion cameras mounted at each end of the bridges captured only curious sniffs.

Then, on a rainy night in {{ first_crossing_month }}, a mother {{ target_arboreal_species }} carrying twins on her back stepped onto Bridge Number {{ specific_bridge_number }} and crossed safely to the other side. Soon, the bridges became bustling aerial highways. # Looking Up When We Build

Today, national infrastructure guidelines in {{ adopting_country_name }} mandate canopy connectivity assessments before any new road or power line is approved in forested regions.
