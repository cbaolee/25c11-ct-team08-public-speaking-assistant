# Introduction: Five Chapters of Ruin -- and the Pen in Our Hand

If you could compress the entire **{{ earth_life_history_billion_years }}-billion-year history of complex life on planet Earth** into a single five-hundred-page book, almost every page would be bursting with weird, wonderful experiments of evolution.

Every hundred pages or so, however, you would turn a page and find it **burned almost completely black**.

Paleontologists call these the **Big Five Mass Extinctions** -- five moments in deep geological history when up to **{{ big_five_max_loss_percentage }} of all species on Earth** were wiped out in a geological blink of an eye by volcanic super-eruptions, shifting continents, or, **sixty-six million years ago**, a ten-kilometer-wide asteroid slamming into the Yucatán Peninsula with the force of a billion atomic bombs.

After each of those five cataclysms, it took evolution between **five million and ten million years** just to rebuild the tree of life.

Now turn to **Page 500** -- the page we are living on right now.

Look around the world today. There is no giant asteroid screaming out of the sky. Super-volcanoes aren't burying continents in lava.

Yet when paleontologists compare the normal "background rate" at which species naturally go extinct in the fossil record -- roughly **{{ background_extinction_rate }} species per million species per year** -- to the rate at which vertebrates, insects, and plants are disappearing today, the current extinction rate is running **{{ current_extinction_multiplier }} to one thousand times faster than normal**.

> For the first time in {{ earth_life_history_billion_years }} billion years, a **Sixth Mass Extinction** is underway -- and this time, **we are the asteroid**.

Wait -- before you sink into your chair in despair, let me tell you the single most important difference between the asteroid of sixty-six million years ago and us today:

**A rock flying through space has no brain, no heart, and no steering wheel.** An asteroid cannot look down at the dinosaurs, realize what it is doing, and choose to change course.

**We can.**

# The Siren Song of De-Extinction

Faced with this crisis, over the past decade a flashy new idea has grabbed magazine covers, venture-capital millions, and Hollywood headlines: **De-Extinction**.

Biotechnology companies and synthetic biologists are using **CRISPR gene editing** and ancient DNA extracted from permafrost bones and museum skins in an effort to engineer proxies of long-gone icons: splicing **{{ woolly_mammoth_genes_count }} cold-adaptation genes** from the extinct **Woolly Mammoth** into the cells of an Asian elephant, or attempting to reconstruct the genome of the **{{ dodo_or_thylacine_example }}**.

Now, as a scientist, I will be the first to admit that the molecular technology behind ancient DNA and gene editing is **breathtaking**. And as we have seen with genetic rescue for living species like the **{{ black_footed_ferret_or_rhino_example }}**, using biotechnology to inject lost genetic diversity back into *living* endangered species is a genuine game-changer.

Yet when people ask me, *"Hey, if we can just bring the Mammoth and the Dodo back in a lab, does extinction even matter anymore?"*, we need to have a very honest, clear-eyed conversation about **three hard truths**.

**Truth Number One: You are not actually bringing the extinct species back.**

Even if you edit sixty or a hundred mammoth hair-and-fat genes into an Asian elephant cell -- out of a genome of **three billion base pairs** -- what walks out of the surrogate womb is not a true *Mammuthus primigenius*. It is a **hairy transgenic elephant**!

And who teaches a baby mammoth how to be a mammoth when every single mammoth mother and matriarch died four thousand years ago?

**Truth Number Two: Where are they supposed to live?**

Why did the **{{ extinct_modern_species_example }}** go extinct in the first place? Because its forest was chopped down, its river was dammed, or its island was overrun by rats! If you spend fifty million dollars hatching a proxy of an extinct bird in a petri dish while its home habitat is still a parking lot or a palm-oil plantation, **all you have created is a very expensive prisoner for a zoo cage**.

**Truth Number Three: The Opportunity Cost in the Emergency Room.**

Conservation runs on a shoestring budget; the entire world currently spends less on saving all endangered species on Earth than Americans spend on **{{ trivial_spending_comparison }}** in a single year!

When my colleagues modeled the economics of de-extinction in {{ cost_study_location }}, they found that for the **exact same price** it takes to keep just one single "de-extinct" species alive in the wild for ten years, traditional field conservation could save **between {{ living_species_saved_multiplier_min }} and {{ living_species_saved_multiplier_max }} living endangered species** that are still breathing on Earth right now!

> De-extinction is fascinating science, but it must never become an excuse to look away from the **one million real, original masterpieces** begging for our help on the Red List today. Prevention is a thousand times cheaper, kinder, and truer than resurrection.

# The Four Levers That Can Stop the Sixth Extinction

So if we are the first species in Earth's history that can see a mass extinction coming *and* the only species that can stop it, **what is the battle plan?**

Over the past five years, global biodiversity modeling by {{ global_science_panel_name }} proved that we can **bend the curve of biodiversity loss** before {{ target_turnaround_year }} if we pull four practical levers together:

**Lever One: Protect the "Half-Earth" Hotspots and 30x30.**

Species are not spread evenly across the planet like butter on toast! Fully **{{ biodiversity_in_hotspots_percentage }} of the world's rarest, most endangered land vertebrates and plants** are concentrated in just **{{ hotspot_land_area_percentage }} of the Earth's land surface** -- in tropical cloud forests, islands, and biodiversity hotspots like {{ hotspot_examples_list }}.

By legally protecting **30% of the planet's land and oceans by 2030** -- targeting those exact irreplaceable **Alliance for Zero Extinction (AZE)** sites where a species lives on only one single mountain or bay on Earth, and placing legal land titles and direct funding into the hands of **Indigenous peoples** who already guard {{ indigenous_biodiversity_share }} of the world's biodiversity -- we can take **over {{ extinction_risk_drop_percentage }} of Red-Listed species** out of immediate danger in one stroke!

**Lever Two: Fix How We Feed Ourselves.**

Right now, agriculture takes up **half of all the habitable land on Earth**, and **{{ livestock_land_share_percentage }} of all agricultural land** is used for livestock grazing and feed crops -- while humanity throws away **one-third of all the food we grow** into the garbage!

By cutting food waste in half, shifting toward more plant-rich diets, and paying farmers to practice **regenerative, wildlife-friendly farming** that boosts yields on existing farmland while weaving hedgerows and wetlands back through the countryside, we can feed ten billion people *and* give **{{ returned_habitat_million_hectares }} million hectares** of land back to wild nature.

**Lever Three: Shut Down the Three Direct Killers.**

Look at why Red-Listed species die even inside protected parks: **invasive species, commercial poaching, and bycatch**.

We already have the tools to stop all three! We have proven we can clear invasive rats and predators off **{{ restored_islands_count }} islands**; we have K9 units, eDNA forensics, and community guardians shutting down poaching syndicates; and we have five-dollar streamer lines and ropeless gear that slash marine bycatch by ninety percent. All it takes is scaling what already works.

**Lever Four: Rewire the Global Economy to Value Living Nature.**
