Biologists call this insular woodiness: when a tiny, soft herb lands on a remote island or mountain peak where there are no trees, evolution says, "Hey, the tree job is wide open!" -- and turns a daisy, a lobelia, or a violet into a towering giant that looks like it belongs on another planet.

For millions of years, the extreme height and cold of {{ argument_island_or_sky_island_location }} kept these alien mountain gardens safe. Because there were no large hoofed mammals on oceanic islands like {{ argument_volcanic_peak_name }}, plants like the {{ argument_target_giant_rosette_species }} never bothered to evolve thorns, tough bark, or bitter chemical poisons -- putting all their energy into surviving the cold and dry wind instead.
