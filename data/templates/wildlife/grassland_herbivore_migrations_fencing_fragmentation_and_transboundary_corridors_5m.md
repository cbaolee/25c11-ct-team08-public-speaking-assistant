# Mapping the Memory of the Herd

How do animals know where to go across featureless plains? Using solar-powered GPS collars on {{ collared_individual_count }} lead females, our research revealed that migration routes are **cultural memories**.

Young calves learn the exact mountain passes, river crossings, and mineral licks from their mothers. Once a herd is blocked for a single generation and the elders die off, that collective geographical memory is erased forever, even if you tear the fence down later.

# Tearing Down Barriers Across Borders

To save the {{ migrating_herbivore_species }} of {{ study_region_name }}, we didn't need to buy millions of hectares of land. Because the herds only use a specific corridor for {{ passage_duration_timeframe }} each year, we needed **dynamic coexistence**.

We replaced lethal wire with wildlife-friendly fencing, built highway underpasses, and created a compensation fund that pays pastoralists to lower their boundary gates during the {{ seasonal_migration_month }} migration window.
